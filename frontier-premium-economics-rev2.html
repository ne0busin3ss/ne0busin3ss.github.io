Why Enterprises Pay the Frontier Premium

Mechanisms behind the closed-vs-open procurement decision · modelled economics · September version · supersedes the August edition
THE ARGUMENT Token price is the wrong denominator. Model spend runs ~1–2% of loaded labour (structural, HIGH), and on threshold-gated work delivered value is convex rather than linear in capability (modelled, MED). Over long horizons per-step reliability compounds — pN is the upper bound on that decay, not a measurement of it, since real failures correlate and harnesses recover (section 1).

What changed since August is the open-weights ledger. Under this model's throughput assumption, self-hosting does not break even against a third-party open-weights API at any utilisation the fleet can reach — the escape hatch is renting open weights, not running them. That rests on 5,000 output tok/s per node, tagged LOW and the swing variable throughout. The genuine overpayment isn't choosing proprietary; it's routing everything to a flagship max-effort tier.

Correction of record. The August edition claimed a calibration advantage for the frontier tier; an interim draft of this version reversed it on newer hallucination rates. Both were wrong and both were this document's — the metric is conditional on non-correct answers and neither checked the denominator. Driver 4 is withdrawn, not flipped (section 5).
ABSTRACT — WHAT CHANGED SINCE THE AUGUST EDITION
Four load-bearing inputs moved. The thesis survives all four and reads stronger.
1
Reference prices were two generations stale CORRECTED
The $75/1M flagship line was legacy Opus 4.1 pricing. Frontier output now runs $25 (Opus 5) to $50 (Fable 5). Sonnet 5 held its introductory $2/$10 as standard — the 1 September increase was cancelled. Every reference line in section 2 is redrawn.
2
Self-hosting lost its case entirely REVISED
Hosted open-weights output fell from the assumed $6/1M floor to a ~$1.85 provider median. Four nodes flat out for a year produce ~631B output tokens worth ~$1.17M of API spend against $1.81M fixed — no utilisation at which the modelled fleet wins. The mid-tier crossover moved to 28.7–34.3%, now inside the 20–35% real band.
3
The calibration driver was a denominator error WITHDRAWN
The rate is incorrect ÷ all non-correct responses, so abstaining deflates it. Decomposed per 100 questions, absolute wrong assertions span only 20.1 to 27.9 across a lineup running 40–65% accuracy. Sonnet 5's "good" 39.4% yields 23.6 wrong answers — more than Fable 5's "bad" 63.6% at 22.3. The frontier tier is not less reliable; it attempts more and declines less.
4
The composite index caught up to pN REFRAMED
Intelligence Index v4.1 is roughly 50% agentic weight (GDPval-AA v2 20%, Terminal-Bench 2.1 16%, τ³-Banking 14%), GDPval turn limit raised 100→250. The mechanism is unchanged; the claim that composites hide it is now only partly true.
NET The escape hatch from the frontier premium is renting open weights, not running them — the self-hosted quadrant has narrowed to a data-residency decision rather than an economic one. The overpayment that exists remains failure to segment traffic; the calibration finding does not add a second form, because absolute error is too flat across the lineup for model choice to be the lever. Ground the fact-assertion work; route the long-horizon work by pN.

WHY THIS DOMINATES Moving per-step reliability from 97% to 99% is a 2.8× swing at N=50 and 7.4× at N=100 — the boundary between "the agent ships the PR" and "an engineer babysits and re-drives." No token saving recovers a workflow that doesn't complete.
REVISED — WHAT THE MECHANISM DOES AND DOESN'T CLAIM The benchmark caught up. The August edition called this mechanism invisible to composite indices. Intelligence Index v4.1 reweighted toward agentic workloads — GDPval-AA v2 at 20%, Terminal-Bench 2.1 at 16%, τ³-Bench Banking at 14%, roughly half the index — with the GDPval turn limit raised from 100 to 250 and IFBench removed for saturation. v4.1.1 is the current patch; Opus 5 holds #1 at Index 63. Composites now hide the mechanism less than they did three months ago.

And a limit on the curve above. pN assumes independent Bernoulli steps. Real agent failures correlate — a bad plan poisons every subsequent step — and real harnesses recover, which is exactly what τ³-Banking's unhappy-path grading and the raised GDPval turn limit are built to measure. So the chart is an upper bound on decay, not a forecast of completion: the arithmetic is exact (HIGH) but its correspondence to observed agent behaviour is not (MED). The ordering survives — higher per-step reliability wins by a widening margin over horizon — but the specific 2.8× and 7.4× multipliers should be read as the shape of the effect, not as predicted completion rates for a given harness.

REVISED — THE CROSSOVER DISAPPEARED The August edition put the open-weights crossover at 47.9% utilisation against a $6/1M reference. That reference no longer exists in the market. GLM-5.2 lists at $1.40/$4.40 with a provider median nearer $0.55/$1.85, because open weights let third parties compete on hosting; DeepSeek V4 Pro sits at $0.435/$0.87, roughly 78% below Kimi K2.6 on output; DeepSeek V4 Flash is $0.14/$0.28. At a $1.85 median, four nodes running flat out for a year produce ~631B output tokens worth ~$1.17M of API spend against $1.81M of fixed cost. There is no utilisation at which the modelled fleet wins. The mid-tier line moved the other way: 28.7% (A) to 34.3% (B) lands inside the 20–35% real band rather than below it, so self-hosting no longer cleanly beats mid-tier proprietary across real enterprise load — it straddles.
MODEL ASSUMPTIONS — LOAD-BEARING, TAGGED Scenario A — infra $911k/yr (4 nodes × $26/hr — MED) plus ops $900k/yr (3 FTE × $300k loaded — MED) = $1.81M/yr fixed, as originally published. Scenario B — H200 median on-demand is $4.50/GPU-hr as of 28 August 2026, flat over 90 days, giving $36/hr per 8-GPU node: infra $1.26M/yr, $2.16M/yr fixed (MED). The original $26/hr sits near the verified floor ($3.00/GPU-hr), not the median. Peak capacity of 5,000 output tok/s per node for a ~100B-class MoE under heavy batching remains LOW and is still the swing variable — halve throughput and every crossover doubles. The qualitative result survives that in one direction only: self-hosting beats a flagship price early, and beats a hosted open-weights API not at all.

3 · The labour denominator

Throughput gain required to repay the frontier premium · $250k loaded labour per seat · arithmetic unchanged, threshold harder to reach

The premium is repaid in productivity, not tokens. Hover a bar for the monthly output-token volume each spend level implies at current flagship pricing of $25/1M.

THE BAND WHERE THIS IS A NON-DECISION — NOW WIDER At a realistic per-seat delta of $200–500/month, break-even is a 1–2.4% throughput gain — below measurement noise, which is exactly why enterprises stop optimising here. The argument only becomes live above ~$2,000/seat/month, where a 9.6% lift is required. The flagship price cut from $75 to $25 per 1M output pushed that threshold further out: reaching $2,000/seat/month now takes ~80M output tokens per seat per month, against ~27M under the old reference.

4 · Threshold-gated value

Delivered value vs capability · the composite-average trap, priced · illustrative

"Open weights reach ~87% of proprietary" assumes linear value transfer. On work gated by a pass threshold delivered value is sigmoid, and the last few index points sit on the knee.

READING IT On this curve 87% of frontier capability delivers ~39% of the value. The benchmark isn't wrong — a mean over nine or ten evals is simply the wrong functional form for threshold-gated work. The easy 80% of tasks were automatable two model generations ago; the residue is exactly what sits on the knee. Caveat added this revision: the 87% figure is the claim under test, not a verified current ratio. No independently verified open-weights ratio against Intelligence Index v4.1.1 is in hand, and the v4.1 reweighting toward agentic tasks would likely move it. Curve is LOW on curvature, HIGH on direction; the 87% input is MED and aging.

5 · Calibration — what the rate actually measures

AA-Omniscience decomposed into correct / asserted-wrong / abstained per 100 questions · rewritten — the August driver was wrong and so was its first correction

The August edition cited a hallucination-rate gap as a frontier-premium driver. An interim draft of this version flipped that driver on newer rates. Both readings misuse the metric. AA defines hallucination rate as incorrect ÷ (incorrect + partial + not attempted) — a conditional share, not a share of output. Abstaining deflates it mechanically, so the rate cannot be compared across models that attempt different amounts. The decomposition below restores the denominator; hover a bar for the rate and Index behind it.

THE RESULT — ABSOLUTE ERROR IS NEARLY FLAT Across a lineup spanning 40–65% accuracy and a 1.6× spread in hallucination rate, absolute wrong assertions vary only 1.4× — 20.1 to 27.9 per 100 questions. Sonnet 5's flattering 39.4% rate produces 23.6 wrong assertions, more than Fable 5's alarming 63.6% rate produces at 22.3 — because Sonnet 5 declines 36 of every 100 questions and Fable 5 declines 13. Fable 5 dominates Sonnet 5 on both axes at once: 25 points more accuracy and slightly fewer false assertions. The AA-Omniscience Index, defined as correct minus incorrect, is the single number that already nets this out: Fable 5 at 43 against Sonnet 5 at 17.
WHAT THE ROUTING INSTRUCTION ACTUALLY IS The frontier tier is not disqualified for anything. Hallucination rate is a closed-book recall property and says nothing about per-step agentic reliability or reasoning quality — those are Terminal-Bench 2.1, τ³-Banking, GDPval-AA v2 and GPQA/HLE/CritPt, which the frontier tier leads. Fable 5 tops AA-Briefcase for agentic knowledge work at 1,583 Elo against Sonnet 5 at 1,388 and Opus 4.8 at 1,354; Opus 5 leads Vals Index v2 at 67.2% and posts 96.0% on SWE-bench Verified. Three real instructions follow. One: Opus 4.8 is the genuine low-absolute-error pick at 20.1 wrong per 100 — but buys it with 16 points of accuracy, so it suits verification and checking, not research. Two: the fix for ungrounded fact assertion is grounding, not a model swap — the absolute-error band is too narrow for routing to move the needle. Three: a low rate bought by abstention is a trap; Cohere's Command A+ posts 14% hallucination alongside 9% accuracy, which is the reductio.

LANE 1 · OPEN WEIGHTS

Bounded, high-volume, private

Classification, extraction, routing, embedding. Error-tolerant and short-horizon. The revision narrows this lane's self-hosted form and widens its rented form: at $0.28–$1.85 per 1M output the hosted route wins on cost, and self-hosting is now a data-residency or air-gap decision rather than an economic one.

LANE 2 · MID-TIER PROPRIETARY

General reasoning, good-enough, well-calibrated

Where the dominated-flagship trap lives. Sonnet 5 held its $2/$10 introductory rate as standard pricing and scores 55 on AAII v4.1.1. Note what it is not: a lower-error model. It asserts 23.6 wrong answers per 100 against Fable 5's 22.3, and only looks careful because it declines three times as often.

LANE 3 · FRONTIER

Long-horizon agentic, threshold-gated

Agentic chains and work sitting on the sigmoid knee. pN is load-bearing and the token cost is the small line item. Fable 5 leads AA-Briefcase at 1,583 Elo and Opus 5 leads Vals Index v2 at 67.2%, so nothing in the calibration data narrows this lane. Ungrounded fact assertion is the one job to hand a retrieval layer rather than a bigger model.

NET Enterprises don't pay the premium because they're bad at arithmetic — they pay it because the arithmetic they're doing has a different denominator. That holds. What the revision changes is where the open-weights win actually sits: not in self-hosting, which no longer clears a hosted API at any utilisation, but in renting open weights for bounded work at a fifth to a twentieth of mid-tier proprietary cost. The overpayment that exists is still failure to segment traffic. What the calibration work adds is a warning about the diagnosis rather than a new lane: a hallucination rate compared across models that attempt different amounts is not a reliability measurement, and reading one that way yields a routing rule that moves ~3 wrong answers per 100 while giving up 25 points of accuracy. Segment by horizon and by whether the work is grounded — not by a conditional rate.
